About This Tracker

Cabinet-Level Accountability documents prosecution exposure and other accountability-relevant conduct by current federal officials. Entries alleging an actual legal violation are framed to that legal standard, laying out the elements that would need to be proven and mapping specific evidence to each one. Conduct that's lawful but still accountability-relevant — a pardon, a personnel removal, an appointment — is labeled as an accountability concern instead, never dressed up as an alleged violation.

This tracker prioritizes primary sources — court dockets, official statements, Inspector General findings — where they exist, relying on investigative and mainstream reporting where they don't. Because court proceedings, investigations, and congressional actions evolve continuously, entries here are among the most actively rechecked on the site.

Sweep Process

A Cabinet-Level Accountability sweep is the systematic, multi-stage audit and verification pipeline that keeps these records accurate, corroborated, and legally sound as the underlying legal and political situations develop.

Stage 1: Multi-Factor Staleness & Exposure Detection

The sweep begins with an automated scanning script.

The script audits every JSON entry in the datastore against four distinct trigger conditions:

  • Active Legal Keywords: Flags entries whose status field contains: "pending", "ongoing", "investigation", "litigation", or "appeal".
  • Contested Status: Flags entries where contested is explicitly set to true.
  • Low or Moderate Confidence: Flags any entry where confidence_note indicates open evidentiary gaps.
  • Calendar Age Check: Flags entries where last_updated exceeds 60 days — a backstop to periodically recheck records that aren't forced to update by one of the other built-in mechanisms.

Stage 2: Batching & Deep-Dive Primary Research

To prevent superficial skimming, flagged entries are partitioned into controlled batches of approximately 6 files. For each entry in the batch:

  • Full Context Ingestion: The engine reads the official's identity, the underlying statute (such as the Hatch Act, Antideficiency Act, Impoundment Control Act, or FACA), the legal elements required, and existing citations.
  • Targeted Primary-Source Searches: 1 to 3 targeted web searches are conducted per entry against court dockets, congressional committee records, agency inspector general findings, and investigative reporting.
  • Three-Tier Update Classification:
  • Light Touch: No material status change. A new confirming source is appended to evidence[], the confidence_note is refreshed, and last_updated is bumped.
  • Moderate Update: Substantive new legal milestones occurred (e.g., motion filed, subpoena issued, or hearing held). The status and evidence[] arrays are expanded with verified facts.
  • Real Correction: The entry was factually superseded (e.g., a lawsuit was dismissed or a policy rescinded). The entry is updated directly, and a dedicated research brief is generated as a file documenting the search trail and discarded near-misses.

Stage 3: Programmatic JSON Mutation & Schema Validation

Updates are written programmatically via Python scripts that enforce strict schema constraints:

  • Preserves Unicode formatting (literal em-dashes and quotes rather than escaped \uXXXX strings).
  • Enforces 2-space indentation and exact field ordering (id, official, offense_category, incident_summary, contested, elements_required, evidence, status, rebuttal_anticipated, comeback, cause, last_updated, date_added, confidence_note, section_summaries).
  • Re-runs the validators, which check that every entry's section_summaries object has non-empty incident_summary and confidence_note values, to confirm zero schema violations or malformed JSON blocks.

Stage 4: Two-Repository Publishing & Live Deployment

The sweep finishes by executing the synchronized two-repository release process:

  • Private Research Repository: Staged, committed with batch-specific metrics, and pushed.
  • Static Site Compilation: The publication engine compiles the raw JSON entries into public, indexed web feeds, recalculates statistics, and updates search tables.
  • Public Web Repository: Changes are committed and pushed, automatically deploying the verified updates to the web portal.
