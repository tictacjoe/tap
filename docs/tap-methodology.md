The Accountability Project tracks documented government actions across five databases.

**The five databases**

1. **Corporate Deregulation** — actions reducing or removing regulatory oversight of corporate/industry activity. Draws most heavily of the three curated trackers on direct government sources — agency filings, official statements — supplemented by investigative and mainstream reporting.
2. **Cabinet-Level Accountability** — documented prosecution exposure and other accountability-relevant conduct (pardons, personnel actions, appointments) for current officials. Entries alleging an actual legal violation are framed to that legal standard; entries that don't allege illegality are labeled as an accountability concern rather than a violation. Prioritizes primary sources — court dockets, official statements — where they exist, relying on investigative and mainstream reporting where they don't.
3. **Government Service Redirection** — changes in how government services are delivered, funded, or reassigned. Leans most on investigative and mainstream reporting of the three curated trackers, with primary government sources used where available.
4. **Reporting** — a running archive of relevant news coverage, distinct in scale and purpose from the three curated trackers above. Sourced almost entirely to established news organizations rather than primary documents, by design.
5. **User Topics** — reader-suggested topics that don't clear the bar for one of the three curated trackers, researched to a lighter standard and given a verdict (Confirmed, Partially Confirmed, Unable to Verify, Misleading as Stated) rather than a quantified impact figure. A topic that later clears a curated tracker's bar gets promoted there, with a note left behind showing where it started.
